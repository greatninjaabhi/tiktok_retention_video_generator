## 2. Per-account teardown

### A. Null Monolith — the benchmark
- **Grid (latest):** 3 pinned hits: "How To Clean Dirty Money" **2.7M**, a "LIVED" video **2.6M**, a "MONEY" video **2.5M**. Recent posts: tax-paying business-owner diagram **19.9K**, "How avoid taxes with luxury watch" **~45K**, "How inflation works" **52K**. Others: Dubai, HMRC hacks, World Cup 2026, "£2.6 Million Cigarette".
- **Pattern:** a few videos reach 2.5M+, and most land at 20–50K. **Roughly 50–100× variance on the same format.** The account is built on outliers.
- **Packaging:** big red/yellow outlined title on every cover, one character, one prop-driven scene, same background, so the grid reads as a series.
- **Topics:** dark-curiosity ("clean dirty money"), "rich people tricks", tax hacks, UK-specific (HMRC, £), occasional news hijacks (World Cup).
- **Distribution:** tags both #uk and #USA. Has a **second account** ("Real World Bears") with the same playbook.
- **Weaknesses:** built on a copyrighted character (takedown/demonetization risk, can't own merch or brand); static-looking AI illustrations; UK-heavy topics leave US Gen Z underserved; "how to clean dirty money" sits close to TikTok's scams/illegal-activity lines.

### B. The squid-character account (owner's first screenshot)
- **Grid:** BILLIONAIRE **669.1K**, PROPERTY **622.6K**, money-in-washer "LOOK" **508.3K**, "Why the poor get poorer" **263.8K**, DESTROY **64.6K**, "How to avoid taxes" **61.5K**, AIRPORTS **37.5K**, "Why all rich prefer Dubai" **28.8K**, BILLIONAIRE (2) **23.3K**. Average ≈ 250K across 9.
- **Same template as A:** same background, same washer/laundering idea, same Dubai topic, same title style. **This is a repeatable AI-illustration template being run across multiple accounts, with the character swapped.** Expect more copycats.
- **Takeaway:** the template works, but it's being commoditized. Differentiation has to come from character, animation quality and topic selection, not the layout.

### C. Dirty Dollars — scale through series
- 556.9K followers, but the recent "PART 424 → 419" videos show **5.6K, 3.2K, 52.6K, 2.9K, 5.6K, 2.1K**.
- **Lesson 1:** a big follower count doesn't carry views on TikTok. Every video earns its reach from the For You feed. Low recent views suggest **series fatigue** after 400+ near-identical parts and identical covers.
- **Lesson 2:** the "unethical ways to get rich (for educational purposes)" positioning is a **policy risk**. TikTok bans promoting or teaching scams and fraud (see brief §5.4). Don't copy the framing.
- **Lesson 3 (positive):** an **original** simple doodle character reached ~557K followers. Original characters can win in this niche.

### D. how really? — style inconsistency costs consistency
- Views by video (blurry capture, approximate): 110.3K, 18.2K ("Funds explained in 60 seconds"), 15.9K ("Why the rich love real estate"), 266.9K ("Watch before buying crypto"), **1.5M** ("Nobody explains stocks like this"), 4.6K; second row ~2.3K–3.9K ("Top 10 movies about money", "What it feels like to be broke", "What it feels like to earn $50–100K").
- **Pattern:** one 1.5M hit, most videos at 2–4K. Mixed visuals (doodles, stock photos, movie stills) make it **look like a different channel every video**.
- **Funnel:** bio sends viewers to YouTube for "complete uncensored videos", using TikTok as a funnel to long-form.
- **Lesson:** "Nobody explains X like this" and "Watch before buying Y" hooks beat listicles ("Top 10 movies") by ~100×.

### E–H. Smaller same-niche accounts
- Mr Collins Finance (~190K followers) ran "That's why billionaires never do this" to ~15K likes. Tom Finance and Stickman Finance get hundreds to low thousands of likes.
- **Lesson:** the "billionaire secrets" topic alone isn't enough. Execution (character appeal, hook, visuals, pacing) separates 2M from 2K.

### Adjacent: monetization models seen
- Conspiracy PeterX bio sells **ebooks and a Skool community**. how really? funnels to **YouTube**. Null Monolith runs a **second account**.
- The money in this niche isn't only Creator Rewards. It's funnels (products, communities, YouTube) plus multiple accounts.

### Market signal: saturation
- Several of these accounts (C, D and the PeterX account) appeared together in a YouTube tutorial about an "AI side hustle". **This format is being actively taught, so copycats will keep arriving.** Being generic won't survive; being the most recognizable, best-animated, most accurate version will.

## 3. Patterns across winners

1. **Outlier-driven.** Every account shows 1–3 videos at 0.5–2.7M and most at 2–60K. Plan for a low median and design the system to **find and repeat outliers fast** (brief §8).
2. **Winning topics:**
   - Dark curiosity: dirty money, tax "hacks", "how the rich avoid…"
   - Rich vs poor: "why the poor get poorer", billionaire habits
   - Property / real estate
   - "Nobody explains X like this", "Watch before buying X"
   - Feelings about money: "what it feels like to be broke"
3. **Weaker topics:** location lifestyle (Dubai, airports), listicles ("top 10 movies"), dense diagrams (the business-structure diagram at 19.9K).
4. **Packaging:** one character + one prop per scene; big red/yellow outlined cover title; a single keyword on screen; constant background, so the grid looks like a show.
5. **Color.** The winners are **full-color** illustrations (colored character, colored props, tinted background). The pure black-and-white stickman accounts we found are small. *(Observation from a small sample. Treat it as a hypothesis to test, not a fact.)*
6. **IP risk is everywhere.** The two biggest exact matches use copyrighted TV cartoon characters. That's their biggest weakness and our opening.

| Their weakness | Our counter |

| No visible retention strategy past 60 s | Re-hook at 25–30 s, open loops, 61–75 s scripts (brief §2.5) |

## 5. What to copy vs avoid

**Copy (patterns, not content):**
- Character + one prop-driven visual metaphor per beat.
- Big outlined title on cover; single-keyword on-screen text.
- Constant background color across videos (grid branding).
- Dark-curiosity and rich-vs-poor topic angles (legal framing).
- Pinning the top 3 outliers to the profile.
- A funnel (YouTube long versions, newsletter, product) once there's an audience.

**Avoid:**
- Any existing cartoon/TV character or celebrity likeness.
- "Unethical ways to get rich" and how-to-commit-fraud framing.
- Identical covers across hundreds of parts.
- Mixed visual styles.
- Dense diagrams that need pausing to read.

## 6. Ongoing competitor tracking (feeds the picker)

- **Weekly:** screenshot each tracked account's grid with the **"Popular" tab** selected (shows all-time top hits) and the **"Latest" tab** (shows current baseline). Drop the images in `inbox/competitors/`; the tracker extracts title, keyword and view counts (vision model + human check).
- **Per video logged:** account, followers at capture, views, title/hook, topic tag, hook type, character/style, date. Compute an **outlier score = views ÷ that account's median recent views**.
- **Weekly report:** top 10 outliers across accounts, recurring winning topics/hooks, new entrants.
- Watchlist to start: A, B, C, D, E, plus any new account found via `#finance #animation` tags.
