# Writer brief: White House overdose-credit claim, fact-checked (2026-11-18)

Written by Claude in an unattended scheduled run (no `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, so not `npm run draft`). Modeled on the Two Dan Sullivans brief.

## Story

On Nov. 16, 2026, the White House drug policy office (ONDCP, director Sara Carter) put out "U.S. Drug Overdose Deaths Decrease for 18th Consecutive Month Under Trump Administration", subtitled "Overdose Deaths Plunge 42% from Biden Era Peak". It cites CDC predicted provisional data: 65,724 deaths in the 12 months ending June 2026, about 41.5% below the peak of 112,418, the lowest since December 2016, and "drug overdose deaths have declined every month since President Trump took office in January 2025." Carter credits border security, "dismantling and eliminating narco-terrorist organizations" and prevention. On Nov. 18, NPR reported the claims as misleading about timing: by CDC data the decline began in 2023 and has run at least 39 straight months.

## Why this one

Fresh (release Nov. 16, NPR fact-check Nov. 18). A real fact-check with a primary-source claim and primary-source data to check it against, after several explainers in a row (Mullin, Van Hollen/Schumer, Colom). Opens on Trump, after openers on Mullin, Van Hollen and Colom. The "absurd but true" detail: the headline counts 18 months of a streak that's at least 39 months long.

## Proven (primary source, or two or more outlets)

- The White House claim, headline and numbers (whitehouse.gov release, primary).
- Deaths at their lowest in about a decade: WH ("lowest ... since December 2016"); NPR ("lowest level in roughly a decade").
- About 50,000 lives a year: NPR ("saving nearly 50,000 lives each year"). Said as NPR's figure.
- Decline started in 2023: NPR (CDC data, "began in 2023"), AJMC on CDC data ("declined annually since 2023"), PolitiFact (fentanyl supply drop and death decline starting 2023), Brookings' Felbab-Brown via NPR ("since the spring of 2023").
- 2024 deaths fell 26.9% from 2023 (CDC/NCHS press release, May 14, 2025: 80,391 vs 110,037). Biden was president all of 2024.
- 2025 deaths fell about 14% (AJMC on CDC data, May 2026). So 2024 was the bigger drop.
- Most of the drop came before Trump: NPR ("most of the gains occurring before Trump returned to office"; "roughly one-third" by the time Biden left). CDC math: 112,418 → 80,391 by end of 2024 is about 32,000 of the 46,700 total drop.
- Deaths spiked in Biden's first two years (NPR); the peak was under Biden (WH release itself).
- Deaths kept falling under Trump (NPR, experts quoted; WH; AJMC).
- Causes are a mix: naloxone, treatment, less fentanyl in the supply (PolitiFact; NPR).
- China moved against fentanyl precursor chemicals in late 2023 after Biden pressure: NPR (Biden-Xi meeting, Woodside, Nov. 2023); PolitiFact (Science study: crackdown "partly followed Biden administration pressure").

## Claims and quotes (said as theirs)

- WH credits the border and cartel crackdown (release, primary).
- Carter to NPR: Biden "doesn't get to take credit for ... bringing them down from their own failure." Paraphrased in the script, no quote marks; only NPR has it.
- Felbab-Brown (Brookings) to NPR: "I cannot think of one narco-terrorist organization that has been dismantled." Paraphrased, attributed to NPR; only NPR has it.

## What complicates each side

- Against our lean: the peak happened under Biden, after a sharp spike in his first two years; deaths have kept falling under Trump; researchers don't hand the credit to any one president, and supply changes may matter more than any policy (PolitiFact).
- Against the White House: the "18 months" framing starts the clock at inauguration, though the streak is at least 39 months; the biggest yearly drop was 2024; Brookings' expert disputes the cartel claim.

## The right's framing

- Fox News (read in full; on CDC data through Aug. 2025) quotes a Penn State adjunct: "The fall begins at the end of the Biden administration," and credits the border. Fox says the decline "accelerated when Trump returned"; CDC annual numbers don't show that (−26.9% in 2024, about −14% in 2025), so it isn't used.
- Both sides grab credit: Biden's White House also claimed the drop in Dec. 2024 (STAT, NPR, per a search summary). Not in the script, but it's why the take is "share the credit".
