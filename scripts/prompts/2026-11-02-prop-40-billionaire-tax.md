# Writer brief: Prop 40, California's billionaire tax, and why Newsom's a no

*explainer · 2026-11-02. Written by Claude in an unattended scheduled run, not by `npm run draft` (no API key in that environment).*

**Angle:** A Democratic governor and Bernie Sanders are on opposite sides of a tax on a couple hundred people. The revenue math is honestly disputed. The spending isn't.

**Who's watching:** Gen Z, most of them not in California. Left-leaning viewers will assume the tax is a free win. Don't give them that: wealth taxes have a bad record, and the backers' own economist says so.

**Feeling to leave them with:** "Oh, it's way messier than tax the rich, yes or no."

**Why they care:** It'd be America's first wealth tax on billionaires, and both camps say other states would follow.

**Funniest true detail:** Jensen Huang, worth about $180 billion, on his $8 billion bill: "I'm just afraid of being poor."

## What's proven, alleged and denied
- **Proven, from the state:** One-time 5% tax on net worth, for billionaires living in California on Jan. 1, 2026. 90% goes to health care. Real estate, pensions and retirement accounts are generally excluded. The Legislative Analyst says tens of billions over several years, and under $1 billion a year lost in income tax.
- **Proven, from filings:** Brin's $102 million. Building a Better California gave more than $304 million against 40 and for 41 and 42 through Oct. 20 (Capital & Main). The yes side raised about $34.3 million (LA Times, via Realtor.com/Yahoo). Meta's filing on the Zuckerberg deal (Bloomberg).
- **Disputed:** $100 billion (Zucman, Saez, Boll) against a net $39 billion loss (Rauh and Jaros, Hoover/Stanford). Rauh counts assumed future income tax losses and halves revenue for court risk. Zucman calls that "absurdly one-sided". Whether Brin and the others really ended their residency: Rauh says yes, Zucman and a drafter say probably not.
- **Complicates the yes side:** Past wealth taxes "by and large, have been abject failures" (Zucman's own book, via NPR). Teachers' and firefighters' unions are opposed. Zucman admits he doesn't really want this to be one-time. Legal experts say lawmakers could make some amendments without voters.
- **Complicates the no side:** Ads say it could spread to everyone. CalMatters: it's written into the Constitution, so expanding it would likely take another vote, though it could end up in court. The state analyst's income tax loss is far below Rauh's. Huang says he's fine paying.

## Hooks considered
1. **Gavin Newsom's voting no on one tax. Guess who'd pay it.** (picked: recognizable name, holds back the answer, true: he announced he's a no)
2. Bernie Sanders wants this. Gavin Newsom's fighting it. Guess what it is. (good, but less of a question about the money)

4. Mark Zuckerberg told his board he might leave California. Here's what they did. (a side plot, not the story)
5. Jensen Huang says paying eight billion is a privilege. Other billionaires disagree. (spends the joke)

## Voice for this story
- No prediction about the result, and no telling people how to vote. Nothing on how or where to vote.
- "Tuesday" rather than "tomorrow", so it holds in any slot before polls close.

## Avoid
- "Fled". Say who lists what address, and that residency is disputed.
- Saying Newsom's taking billionaire money or doing it for them. Nothing read shows that. His stated reason gets said.
- Calling Rauh's or Zucman's number the right one.

## Sample lines
- The math's disputed. Who's paying to stop it isn't.
- He's worth over a hundred eighty billion. "I'm just afraid of being poor."
