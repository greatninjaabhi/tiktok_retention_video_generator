# Writer brief: Markwayne Mullin flies to LSU-Texas the day after the FAA ground stop (2026-11-17)

Written by Claude in an unattended scheduled run (no `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, so not `npm run draft`). Modeled on the Two Dan Sullivans brief.

## Story

On Friday, Nov. 13, the FAA ordered a nationwide ground stop (about 3:30 to 8:15 p.m. ET, FAA). That night Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin and Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy briefed reporters at the White House. On Saturday, Nov. 14, after flights had resumed but while many travelers were still stranded, Mullin went to the Coast Guard Academy vs. Merchant Marine Academy game in D.C., then flew on a government plane to the LSU-Texas game in Baton Rouge. The New York Post broke it Tuesday, Nov. 17; Mullin defended it on CNBC the same day.

## Why this one

Fresh (broke Nov. 17), covered left (CNN, HuffPost, New Republic), center (AP/PBS, ABC) and right (NY Post, Fox News). A Republican cabinet member, after a Democratic-leadership video yesterday. Absurd true detail: a college football doubleheader the day after he explained the meltdown, with the first game being his own department's Coast Guard. Different format from the last few: a "what happened next" day-in-the-life, not a vote count.

## Proven (on the record, two or more outlets or a primary source)

- Ground stop Nov. 13, ~3:30 to 8:15 p.m. ET (FAA Q&A); "more than four hours" (AP, ABC).
- Over 1.5 million travelers affected (CNN; Fox "more than 1.5 million travelers on Friday alone").
- Mullin briefed at the White House with Duffy that Friday night (CNN, AP, Fox, ABC photo caption).
- Mullin on Friday blamed new equipment overwhelming the old system (NY Post, Fox, verbatim).
- Saturday flights were back in the air, but travelers were still stranded (AP; ABC "well into Saturday").
- Two games Saturday: Coast Guard Academy vs. Merchant Marine Academy at Audi Field, then LSU-Texas in Baton Rouge on a government plane (NY Post, Fox).
- Coast Guard is a DHS component (dhs.gov).
- DHS: "required by law to travel on a government airplane"; pays personally for non-official flights (NY Post, ABC, Fox; CNN paraphrase).
- Mullin on CNBC, verbatim in CNN, ABC, AP and NY Post: "They'd been completely open for 21 hours."
- "We still got to live our life at the same time" (verbatim in CNN, ABC and NY Post; AP has "gotta live our lives", so the script uses the CNN/ABC/Post wording).
- He says he went for his 12-year-old great-niece, a big LSU fan (ABC, NY Post).
- Tickets came via House Majority Leader Steve Scalise (CNN, ABC, NY Post); Mullin says he pays for all of it (CNN).
- Seen in the private box of Bollinger Shipyards CEO Ben Bordelon (NY Post reported; CNN, ABC, Fox repeat it; Mullin denied knowing him, not being there).
- Bollinger: $2.2 billion contract (CNN: DHS, awarded December, before Mullin was sworn in in March; NY Post and Fox also give $2.2 billion).
- White House official: the trip was "almost a whole day after" the ground stop lifted (CNN); NY Post government source: about 24 hours, no exceptions made.
- Cabinet officials like the secretary of state must fly government jets (NY Post, Fox).

## Alleged / disputed

- That he never met Bordelon and talked no business: his and a source's account (CNN, ABC, NY Post). Said as his.
- "Paid fair market value" for tickets: a source (ABC, NY Post) and Mullin (CNN). Said as his.

## What complicates each side

- Against our lean: airspace had been open for about a day; cabinet secretaries are required to fly government jets; he says he pays for personal trips; the Bollinger contract predates him; the first game was literally his own department's team.
- Against Mullin: he was the public face of the crisis the day before, and travelers were still stuck while he flew to a game and sat in a DHS contractor's box.
